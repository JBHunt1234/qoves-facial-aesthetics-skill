# Workwear & Dress Codes (with a Finance Focus)

Practical notes for dressing for professional settings, especially finance (banking, PE, asset management, sales & trading, wealth management, corporate finance). Dress codes vary by firm, desk, city and seniority. Calibrate to the most senior client-facing person you respect at your firm, then dress one notch sharper for client meetings.

---

## The Dress-Code Ladder

| Level | What it means in finance | Typical uniform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Business formal** | Client pitches, board meetings, interviews, conservative banks, wealth management with older clients | Navy or charcoal suit, white or light-blue shirt, conservative tie, black or dark-brown leather shoes |
| **Business professional** | Suit expected, tie optional | Suit, open collar or tie for meetings, polished leather shoes |
| **Business casual** | Most IB/PE/AM floors today | Tailored trousers or chinos, button-up or oxford shirt, optional blazer or knitwear, leather shoes or loafers |
| **Smart casual** | Casual Fridays, team dinners, offsites, tech-adjacent finance | Blazer or overshirt, knit polo or OCBD, chinos or dark denim (if allowed), suede loafers or clean leather sneakers |
| **Weekend / casual** | Outside work | Personal style with fit and condition still mattering |

**Interviews:** default to a navy or charcoal suit, white or light-blue shirt, and a plain or subtly patterned tie, even if the firm is business casual.

---

## Fit Matters More Than Price

A $400 suit tailored well beats a $2,000 suit off the rack. Points to check:

- **Shoulders:** the seam ends where your shoulder bone ends. No divots, no overhang. This is the hardest thing to alter, so buy for the shoulder.
- **Jacket length:** covers the seat; hem roughly at the knuckles of your relaxed hands.
- **Button stance:** the top (or only) fastened button sits at or slightly above the navel. No X-shaped pulling when buttoned.
- **Sleeves:** show ¼–½ inch of shirt cuff.
- **Trousers:** sit at the natural waist or slightly below. Slight or no break today. A clean leg with a mid-taper, neither skinny nor baggy.
- **Shirt collar:** two fingers fit inside when buttoned. Collar points stay under the jacket lapels.
- **Shirt body:** no billowing at the waist when tucked. A slim or tailored fit if needed, darts if necessary.

Budget an extra $50–150 per suit for tailoring (sleeves, trouser hem, waist suppression).

## Colours and Fabrics

**Suits, in priority order:** navy → charcoal → mid-grey → subtle patterns (pinstripe, windowpane, glen check) once the basics are covered. Avoid black suits for business (they read as evening or funeral) and shiny fabrics.

**Fabric:** worsted wool, 100s–130s for durability. Year-round weight around 260–300 g/m². Flannel in winter. Wool-linen or tropical wool in hot climates.

**Shirts:** white and light blue cover 80% of needs. Then fine stripes, Bengal stripes and small checks. Poplin or twill for formal, oxford cloth for business casual.

**Ties:** solid navy, burgundy or dark green grenadine or silk; repp stripes; small foulard patterns. Width 7–8.5 cm, matched roughly to lapel width. The tip ends at the belt buckle.

**Shoes:** black cap-toe oxford (most formal), dark-brown oxford or derby, brown suede loafers (business casual and smart casual), clean minimal white or tan leather sneakers (smart casual only, firm-dependent). Belt matches shoe colour and finish.

**Socks:** match trouser colour or go one shade darker. Long enough that no shin shows when seated.

**Watch:** slim, simple dial, leather strap or steel bracelet. Understated beats flashy in client-facing roles.

## The "Finance Casual" Uniform and When to Avoid It

The fleece or quilted vest over a button-down (the "Midtown uniform") and the quarter-zip over an oxford are common but can read as a cliché. Sharper alternatives that still fit:

- Merino crewneck or V-neck over an oxford or poplin shirt
- Unstructured navy blazer with an open-collar shirt and grey trousers
- Knit polo with tailored trousers and suede loafers (summer and casual Fridays)
- Cardigan or zip knit in fine-gauge merino instead of fleece

## Dressing for Height and Build

Height and weight are optional inputs. They help with proportions, pattern scale and jacket length. They are never a reason to suggest weight loss.

**Shorter (under about 5'8" / 173 cm):**
- Low-contrast outfits (suit and shirt close in value, matching trousers and socks) make a longer, unbroken line
- Slightly shorter jacket (still covering the seat), higher trouser rise, no break, no turn-ups/cuffs
- Smaller-scale patterns, slimmer lapels and ties to match the frame
- Vertical elements: pinstripes, a single-breasted 2-button jacket with a slightly higher button stance

**Taller (over about 6'1" / 185 cm):**
- Can carry bolder patterns, double-breasted jackets, turn-ups/cuffs and more break
- Make sure sleeves and jacket length are actually long enough (look for "Long" sizes)
- Horizontal interest (contrast belt, layered knitwear) breaks up height if wanted

**Slim / lean build:**
- Texture and layering add presence: flannel, tweed, oxford cloth, knitwear over shirts
- Light shoulder structure, a slightly wider lapel; avoid ultra-skinny cuts that emphasise thinness
- Lighter or mid-tone colours add visual weight

**Athletic / broad shoulders:**
- Prioritise shoulder and chest fit, then take in the waist. Stretch-wool blends help.
- Softer or natural shoulder construction (no extra padding)
- Avoid "athletic fit" shirts that are tight in the arms

**Larger or heavier build:**
- Darker solids and subtle vertical patterns; matte rather than shiny fabrics
- Single-breasted, 2-button, slightly lower button stance for a longer V
- Trousers with a mid or high rise worn at the natural waist; a clean drape rather than a tight taper
- Fit, not size labels: a correctly sized garment looks slimmer than an undersized one
- Open spread collars balance a fuller face and neck

## Face–Clothing Links

- **Collar ↔ face:** longer or narrower faces suit wider spread collars; rounder or wider faces suit point or semi-spread collars
- **Neckline:** a deep V lengthens the face and neck; crewnecks and shirt collars shorten it
- **Lapel and tie width** should roughly match each other and the scale of the person's features
- **Contrast level:** match outfit contrast to the person's natural contrast (hair vs skin vs eyes). High-contrast people suit navy with white and charcoal with crisp white. Low-contrast people suit tonal combinations (navy with light blue, grey with soft blue, brown with cream).
- **Glasses:** metal or thinner frames read more formal. Bold acetate reads creative unless balanced with classic tailoring.

---

## Grooming for Professional Settings

- A haircut every 3–4 weeks keeps a "maintained" look; the neckline and around the ears go first
- Facial hair: clean shaven, or deliberately kept stubble or a short beard with defined lines. Patchy or unkempt growth reads careless in conservative settings.
- Nails trimmed, shoes polished, no lint or pilling. Condition signals as much as cost.

## Quick Outfit Formulas (Finance)

1. **Client pitch:** navy suit · white poplin shirt · navy grenadine tie · black cap-toe oxfords · black belt
2. **Board / formal meeting:** charcoal suit · light-blue shirt · burgundy tie · black oxfords
3. **Everyday business casual:** mid-grey wool trousers · light-blue oxford · navy merino crewneck · dark-brown derbies
4. **Elevated business casual:** navy unstructured blazer · white OCBD · grey trousers · brown suede loafers
5. **Casual Friday:** navy knit polo or OCBD · stone chinos · suede loafers or white leather sneakers
6. **Client dinner:** charcoal or navy suit · open-collar white shirt or fine-knit roll-neck in winter · dark loafers
7. **Offsite / smart casual:** overshirt or chore jacket · fine-gauge knit · dark chinos · clean leather sneakers
